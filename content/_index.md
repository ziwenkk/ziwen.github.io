        I am a postdoctoral researcher at USTC. My research experience includes analyzing [**SDSS DR7**](https://sdss.physics.nyu.edu/vagc/) data to investigate how different types of central galaxies relate to their host dark matter halos in the local universe.  My work also covers AGN triggering mechanisms, AGN–galaxy coevolution scenarios, and galaxy formation efficiency. (Zhang et al. [2021](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2021/06/aa40150-20/aa40150-20.html), [2022](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2022/07/aa42866-21/aa42866-21.html), [2023](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ad0892)). Additionally, I have conducted extensive research employing weak lensing, two-point correlation functions, and satellite kinematics to place constraints on halo masses. Using [**UNIONS**](https://www.skysurvey.cc/) data, I characterized PSF-related systematic effects in weak lensing measurements (Zhang et al. [2024](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2024/11/aa50623-24/aa50623-24.html)). More recently, my research has focused on formation models of dwarf galaxies and their dark matter halos (Zhang et al. [2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08965-5)).

In addition, together with my collaborators, we are planning **observations** to investigate the dark matter halo structures in which dwarf galaxies reside, with the goal of improving our understanding of small-scale structure formation and constraining the cross-section of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM).
